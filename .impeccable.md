## Design Context

### Users
Everyday consumers who want to cover a large wall with a favorite photo without paying for a commercial poster. They are not assumed to understand print-production terminology, image tiling, bleed, trim, or layout software. Their job is to upload an image, arrange ordinary printer-paper sheets into a large composition, preview the physical result, and produce files they can confidently print and assemble at home.

### Brand Personality
Playful, enabling, and approachable. The product should feel like a creative studio rather than office software, while remaining clear enough that a first-time user can reach a printable result without instruction. Privacy should feel inherent: image processing happens locally in the browser, and uploads are never retained or sent to a server.

### Aesthetic Direction
The final palette, typography, and detailed visual language are intentionally deferred until after functional wireframe iteration. Early design should prioritize a legible, low-fidelity studio workspace with a tactile canvas, direct manipulation, and obvious print boundaries. It should avoid looking like a dense professional design suite or a generic upload-and-download utility.

### Design Principles
1. Start with a successful default: a useful poster should be printable after upload with minimal adjustment.
2. Show the physical outcome continuously: paper size, page seams, image crop, and assembled dimensions must remain visible and understandable.
3. Reveal power gradually: basic size and layout choices come first; rotation, custom page arrangements, overlap, and print calibration appear when requested.
4. Prefer direct manipulation backed by precise controls: dragging should feel playful, while snapping, keyboard input, undo, and reset keep it dependable.
5. Keep the image private by architecture: all editing and export happen locally, with concise reassurance near upload and export.
